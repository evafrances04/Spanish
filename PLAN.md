# Eva's plan: the 4,000 most common Spanish words, through listening, in short lessons

*Written 2026-10-17. José follows this plan; Eva just keeps pressing ▶️.*

## The goal: your 4,000 words

**Cover the 4,000 most common Spanish words** in `spanish-top-4000.csv` (your repo): rank, word, part of speech and English meaning, counted from film and TV subtitles. José works through the list **in rank order**, introducing **5 new words** per short listening lesson, inside a story, and recycling the ones you've recently met.

**Your % = how much of the 4,000 you've covered.** A word counts as covered when you've *shown* it, never just because José used it:
1. **You understood it:** it was in 2 pieces, on 2 different days, where you got the gist (José judges your answer, in Spanish, to the gist question against his notes).
2. **Or you used it correctly** in your own messages or voice notes.
3. **Or you marked it as known in the word picker** (`/palabras` on Telegram). You see the upcoming list words as buttons and tap the ones you *don't* know. Those become José's new words; the rest are counted as known. There's no test: everything else comes from what you show in the pieces, and your lessons of 12–17 October already count.

Your 06:00 and 19:00 messages show:
- the % covered;
- how many words you already use;
- how many are "on the way" (heard, not yet shown);
- your coverage of the first 1,000 words;
- your recent comprehension;
- your streak, and the date you'll finish the list at your current pace.

Covering these 4,000 words lines up with the research on B2: about 4,000–5,000 word families (Nation; frequency-list compilations).

## How it runs

- **On demand.** There's no daily lesson. Press ▶️ *Siguiente pieza* (or `/siguiente`) whenever you like, and keep going as long as you want. José keeps 3 or more pieces ready ahead of you, so the next one is instant.
- **06:00:** your % and yesterday's progress. **19:00:** today's pieces, list words gained, your %, your streak, and the date you'll finish the list at this week's pace. Ask "¿cómo voy?" (or `/progreso`) any time.
- **14:00:** no message any more. Replays are built into the pieces instead.

## Your marks, goals and tools (from 2026-10-20)

- **Commands:** `/lectura` a reading test (word help only after you answer) · `/errores` your accuracy trend and most common mistakes · `/objetivo` today's goal · `/nivel` a full level report · `/progreso` your progress · `/siguiente` the next piece.

Then B2. Everything is still by ear; structures you haven't used yet keep coming back in the B2 pieces.

## A piece (5–8 minutes)

| Type | What's in it | How often |
|---|---|---|
| **Nueva** | New audio (about 1–2½ min) on a topic you know → how much did you understand (%) → one gist question, then a chat about the other parts of the audio → listen two or three times and retell it in your own words (R1) → 3–5 shadowing sentences (slow, then normal) → a speaking question | most pieces |
| **Patrones** (part of a Nueva) | 4–6 spoken examples that differ in one way; you notice the pattern. No rule named unless you ask | when a new structure arrives |
| **Otra vez** (a section inside new pieces) | An earlier audio replayed exactly as before, just to listen (the % question is for the new audio, so your first-listen trend stays honest) | occasionally (at most 1 piece in 5, never two in a row): words come back mainly through new contexts |
| **Canción** | A real song (YouTube search link), 1–3 words from it in context → % | about every 10th |
| **Historia** | A longer story built only from words you already know: pure fluency | about every 15th |

**Variety (from 2026-10-18, your decision):** every piece is about a **different subject**, in detail: a specific TV episode, a film, an Oscars moment, an Olympic race, a Beatles story, a Sondheim show, a human-rights case, a moment in international relations, a Sydney or Cali scene, or a swim. No arcs: a subject comes back only after at least 15 other pieces, and from a new angle. **Repetition comes from the words, not the story:** your new and recent words come back in different topics, which is how you show you really know them. Your own stories (what you tell José) turn up now and then in new settings, never retold the same way. Lighter subjects first, denser ones (*The West Wing*, *Game of Thrones*, human rights, Sondheim) once your first listen is steady at 85% or more. Facts are checked; plots are retold in José's own words. The rotation and the subjects already used are in `topics.md`.

**Level of the pieces (from 2026-10-19, your decision):** pitched just above you, at **B1.1–B1.2**: sentences of about 8–12 words, mixing quick back-and-forth with longer linked lines (*porque, cuando, aunque, mientras, así que, sin embargo, por eso*); about half the pieces tell a story in the past; audio of about 1–2½ minutes (150–300 words). The 🐢 button gives you any audio slower. If your first listen drops below about 80% twice in a row, José steps back towards B1.1; if you're steadily at 90% or more, the pieces move towards B1.2–B2.1.

**Lectura** (from 2026-10-19): every 4th or 5th piece has a short text (80–150 words) on your topics to read silently, no audio, with one gist question (L1). No writing tasks.

**Current focus (from your level report, 2026-10-23):** all through the audio, never as rules, alongside the drill of your four most repeated mistakes. (1) **Listen two or three times, then retell:** after the gist question you tell the story in 3–4 sentences in your own words, and audios you rated 60% or less still come back once; new audios go back to about 250–320 words. (2) **Chunks you copy by ear:** the forms that keep slipping come back said right, in the audio and the Sombra (*el miércoles, en diez días, no tenía tiempo, la parte que más me gustó, el discurso, mucha voluntad, me duele el estómago*), plus the Spanish word where you'd reach for English or French. (3) **Real conversation:** the characters react and hand the turn back (*¿En serio?, ¿Vos qué pensás?*), and the Habla question ends by asking you to ask José something back. (4) **B2 by ear, one at a time:** *a pesar de que, por lo tanto*, *si tuviera…, -ría*, reported speech, and later the subjunctive after *busco un… que…*. (5) **Slightly longer readings** (130–150 words, with an opinion and an inference question). Progress is tracked in PROGRESS.md.

**Rules for every piece:**
- You understand nearly all of it. **5 new words from your list in every piece**, and everything else is familiar.
- 4–8 recently met words are recycled in each new piece.
- An earlier audio is replayed only occasionally; your words come back inside new stories instead.
- In the chat, José shows what you said, a verdict (✅ / 🟡 / ❌) and your sentence corrected in bold, in your own words. The pieces themselves never mark you.
- Every new word comes back in the audio of the next 3 pieces and the next day, and José asks questions that invite you to use the words you've heard but not yet said. Your reports show how many of the new words you have used yourself.
- **Calibration:** if your first-listen % is below about 85, the next pieces get easier. Near 100%, they get harder (longer, faster, more natural speech); the new words stay at 5. If it drops below about 80% twice in a row, the 5 stay but everything around them gets easier.

## Stages (by words of the list covered)

*José picks the level from your comprehension % and gist answers, not from this table alone. The table is the order in which structures and vocabulary are seeded.*

Grammar is never taught as rules. Each structure is **seeded**: it first shows up in a *Patrones* set, then in at least 8 later pieces over at least 3 weeks. José treats it as **absorbed** once you've used it correctly, unprompted, twice. The B1/B2 grammar books are José's private checklist, so nothing is missed.

## How long

It depends on how much you already know. The evening message projects your finish date from your real pace over the last week. As a rough guide: at 5 new words per piece, 2–3 pieces a day is 10–15 new words a day, so every 1,000 words takes roughly 70–100 days at that pace (faster on days you do more). That's an estimate, not a promise.

## Milestones

- **25 / 50 / 75 / 100%** of your 4,000 words: celebrated the moment you cross them.
- **Monthly listening check** (first Sunday): one unseen piece slightly above your level. Your first-listen % is recorded as the trend.
- **At 100%:** a longer listening check at B2 level to confirm, then José proposes a C1 plan.
