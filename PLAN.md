# Eva's plan to the end of B2: listening, on demand, in bite-size pieces

*Written 2026-10-17. José follows this plan; Eva just keeps pressing ▶️.*

## The goal, and how it's measured

**B2 = recognising about 4,500 Spanish word families.** A word family is a base word plus its forms and close relatives (*hablar, hablo, hablaron, hablante* = 1 family).

Where that number comes from:
- Paul Nation suggests about **4,000 words for B2**: 2,000–3,000 high-frequency words plus 1,000–2,000 technical words relevant to the learner. ([Nation, Victoria University of Wellington](https://wgtn.ac.nz/lals/resources/paul-nations-resources/vocabulary-lists/vocabulary-cefr-and-word-family-size/vocabulary-and-the-cefr-docx))
- Frequency-list compilations put B2 at around **5,000**. ([Lingua Libre](https://lingualibre.org/wiki/Help:Why_wordlists_matter%3F))
- We use the midpoint, **4,500**. For listening, about 2,000–3,000 families already give about 95% coverage of spoken language (Schmitt, English data), so comprehension climbs fast in the early stages.

Caveats:
- Most of this research is on English learners, so the Spanish figures are applied by analogy.
- English speakers also start with thousands of shared Latin-derived words (especially legal and formal ones), which these counts don't credit.

**The % in the 06:00 and 19:00 messages = estimated families known ÷ 4,500.** It's calculated by the bot, not guessed by José:
1. **Starting point: a 5-minute vocabulary check (`/nivel`).**
   - 6 lists of 18 words, one list per frequency band (the 1–1,000 most common families, then 1,001–2,000, and so on up to 6,000). Each list mixes real families with 3 invented words.
   - You say which ones you don't know. The share you know per band, corrected for any invented words you claimed to know, estimates your known families in that band. This is the yes/no checklist method (Meara).
   - The frequency ranking comes from film and TV subtitles, so it suits a listening approach (FrequencyWords, OpenSubtitles 2018).
   - It's an estimate, with an error of roughly ±10%.
2. **Then, every piece you finish adds to it.**
   - José lists every word family in each piece's audio.
   - A family counts as **learned** once you've heard it in **3 finished pieces on at least 2 different days**: repeated, spaced exposure.
   - Families you probably already knew (from your check) add less, so nothing is double-counted.
3. **Two extra measures in your report:**
   - **"The 3,000 most frequent"**: your coverage of the core of spoken Spanish.
   - **"Used by you"**: families you've used correctly in your own messages and voice notes. This is your active vocabulary, a smaller subset of what you recognise.

## How it runs

- **On demand.** There's no daily lesson. Press ▶️ *Siguiente pieza* (or `/siguiente`) whenever you like, and keep going as long as you want. José keeps 3 or more pieces ready ahead of you, so the next one is instant.
- **06:00:** your % and yesterday's progress. **19:00:** today's pieces, families gained, your %, your streak, and the date you'll reach B2 at this week's pace.
- **14:00:** no message any more. Replays are built into the pieces instead.

## A piece (5–8 minutes)

| Type | What's in it | How often |
|---|---|---|
| **Nueva** | New audio (45–90 s, later up to 2 min) on a topic you know → how much did you understand (%) → one gist question → 2–3 shadowing sentences (slow, then normal) | most pieces |
| **Patrones** (part of a Nueva) | 4–6 spoken examples that differ in one way; you notice the pattern. No rule named unless you ask | when a new structure arrives |
| **Otra vez** | 2 earlier pieces replayed exactly as before → % → shadowing | every 3rd piece |
| **Canción** | A real song (YouTube search link), 1–3 words from it in context → % | about every 10th |
| **Historia** | A longer story built only from words you already know: pure fluency | about every 15th |

**Rules for every piece:**
- You understand nearly all of it. At most 1–3 new families per piece (later about 1 per 40 words), and everything else is familiar.
- 4–8 recently met words are recycled in each new piece.
- Each audio comes back about 1 day, 4 days and 2 weeks later.
- Mistakes are answered with a natural recast. Nothing is marked.
- **Calibration:** if your first-listen % is below about 85, the next pieces get easier. Near 100% with no new words, they get harder.

## Stages (by estimated families known)

Grammar is never taught as rules. Each structure is **seeded**: it first shows up in a *Patrones* set, then in at least 8 later pieces over at least 3 weeks. José treats it as **absorbed** once you've used it correctly, unprompted, twice. The B1/B2 grammar books are José's private checklist, so nothing is missed.

## How long

It depends on where the check puts you, and the bot's projection updates every evening from your real pace. A rough guide: if you start at about 2,500 families, the remaining 2,000 at 2–3 new families per piece (plus replays) means roughly **700–1,000 pieces, or 80–120 hours**. That's about 3–4 months at an hour a day, or faster if you binge. This is an estimate, not a promise; the evening message tracks the real figure.

## Milestones

- **25 / 50 / 75 / 100%** of the way to B2: celebrated the moment you cross them.
- **Monthly listening check** (first Sunday): one unseen piece slightly above your level. Your first-listen % is recorded as the trend.
- **At 100%:** retake `/nivel` to confirm, then José proposes a C1 plan.
