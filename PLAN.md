# Eva's plan: the 4,000 most common Spanish words, through listening, in short lessons

*Written 2026-10-17. José follows this plan; Eva just keeps pressing ▶️.*

## The goal: your 4,000 words

**Cover the 4,000 most common Spanish words** in `spanish-top-4000.csv` (your repo): rank, word, part of speech and English meaning, counted from film and TV subtitles. José works through the list **in rank order**, introducing 1–3 new words per short listening lesson, inside a story, and recycling the ones you've recently met.

**Your % = how much of the 4,000 you've covered.** A word counts as covered when you've *shown* it, never just because José used it:
1. **You understood it:** it was in 2 pieces, on 2 different days, where you got the gist (José judges your answer to the gist question against his notes; English or Spanish both work).
2. **Or you used it correctly** in your own messages or voice notes.
3. **What you already knew:** your comprehension check (`/chequeo`, about 10 minutes) gives estimated credit. That's 5 texts at rising levels; for each, you say what 10 randomly chosen words mean in context, and the share you get right estimates how much of that part of the list you know. Your lessons of 12–17 October already count too.

Your 06:00 and 19:00 messages show:
- the % covered;
- how many words you already use;
- how many are "on the way" (heard, not yet shown);
- your coverage of the first 1,000 words;
- your recent comprehension;
- your streak, and the date you'll finish the list at your current pace.

Covering these 4,000 words lines up with the research on B2: about 4,000–5,000 word families (Nation; frequency-list compilations).

## How it runs

- **On demand.** There's no daily lesson. Press ▶️ *Siguiente pieza* (or `/siguiente`) whenever you like, and keep going as long as you want. José keeps 3 or more pieces ready ahead of you, so the next one is instant.
- **06:00:** your % and yesterday's progress. **19:00:** today's pieces, families gained, your %, your streak, and the date you'll reach B2 at this week's pace.
- **14:00:** no message any more. Replays are built into the pieces instead.

## A piece (5–8 minutes)

| Type | What's in it | How often |
|---|---|---|
| **Nueva** | New audio (45–90 s, later up to 2 min) on a topic you know → how much did you understand (%) → one gist question → 2–3 shadowing sentences (slow, then normal) | most pieces |
| **Patrones** (part of a Nueva) | 4–6 spoken examples that differ in one way; you notice the pattern. No rule named unless you ask | when a new structure arrives |
| **Otra vez** | 2 earlier pieces replayed exactly as before → % → shadowing | every 3rd piece |
| **Canción** | A real song (YouTube search link), 1–3 words from it in context → % | about every 10th |
| **Historia** | A longer story built only from words you already know: pure fluency | about every 15th |

**Arcs (from 2026-10-17):** pieces come in arcs of 3–5 around one of your topics, in depth: a specific TV episode, a film, an Oscars night, an Olympic race, a Beatles moment, a Sondheim show, a human-rights case. Usually: (1) Eva and Andrés chat about the setup, (2) a retelling of the key scenes, (3) a character or moment in detail, (4) behind the scenes or the real-world context, (5) "what would you have done?". Pieces in an arc share words and names, so they recycle naturally. Lighter arcs first (*The Simpsons*, *Parks and Rec*, *30 Rock*, the Olympics, swimming, the Beatles), denser ones later (*The West Wing*, *Game of Thrones*, human rights, Sondheim). Facts are checked; plots are retold in José's own words. The plan of arcs is in `topics.md`. First arc: *The Simpsons*, "Marge vs. the Monorail" (season 4, episode 12).

**Rules for every piece:**
- You understand nearly all of it. At most 1–3 new families per piece (later about 1 per 40 words), and everything else is familiar.
- 4–8 recently met words are recycled in each new piece.
- Each audio comes back about 1 day, 4 days and 2 weeks later.
- Mistakes are answered with a natural recast. Nothing is marked.
- **Calibration:** if your first-listen % is below about 85, the next pieces get easier. Near 100% with no new words, they get harder.

## Stages (by words of the list covered)

*José picks the level from your comprehension % and gist answers, not from this table alone. The table is the order in which structures and vocabulary are seeded.*

Grammar is never taught as rules. Each structure is **seeded**: it first shows up in a *Patrones* set, then in at least 8 later pieces over at least 3 weeks. José treats it as **absorbed** once you've used it correctly, unprompted, twice. The B1/B2 grammar books are José's private checklist, so nothing is missed.

## How long

It depends on where your check puts you. The evening message projects your finish date from your real pace over the last week. As a rough guide: at 2–3 new words per piece plus replays, every 1,000 words you still need to cover is roughly 400–500 pieces, or 40–60 hours of listening. That's an estimate, not a promise.

## Milestones

- **25 / 50 / 75 / 100%** of your 4,000 words: celebrated the moment you cross them.
- **Monthly listening check** (first Sunday): one unseen piece slightly above your level. Your first-listen % is recorded as the trend.
- **At 100%:** a longer listening check at B2 level to confirm, then José proposes a C1 plan.
