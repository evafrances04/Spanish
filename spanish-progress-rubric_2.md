# Spanish progress rubric: B1 to B2

Instructions for the teaching agent. Use this file to measure Eva's progress from B1 towards B2, skill by skill, and to report it back to her in a consistent way.

## Sources this rubric is based on

- Council of Europe, *Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR)*, especially the global scale, the self-assessment grid and the illustrative scales: https://rm.coe.int/168045b15e
- Instituto Cervantes, *Plan Curricular: Niveles de referencia para el español*, the Spanish-specific inventory of grammar, functions, notions and vocabulary for each CEFR level (B1 and B2 volume). Use it to check what content belongs to each level.

The descriptors below are paraphrased summaries, not the official wording. Where a judgement is close, go back to the official CEFR scales and the Cervantes inventories. Do not treat this file as authoritative over them.

## How to assess

1. **Assess skills separately.** Never give a single overall level. Learners' profiles are often uneven, and Eva's listening is likely to run ahead of her writing.
2. **Assess from tasks, not grammar quizzes.** Eva learns through immersion and pattern recognition and does not retain explicit grammar rules. Judge her on what she can do with real or realistic Spanish (a podcast clip, a news article, a conversation, a message to write). Do not test whether she can name or explain a rule.
3. **Require evidence.** Every score must cite the specific task and what she did or did not manage. Example: "Followed the main argument of a 4-minute RNE segment on housing; missed two implied points." No evidence, no score.
4. **Use the sub-levels.** Score each skill on this scale: `A2+`, `B1.1`, `B1.2`, `B2.1`, `B2.2`, `C1 (emerging)`.
5. **Assess a skill at most once a week.** Daily lessons should not each produce a level. Take a weekly snapshot per skill, so that one good or bad day does not move the trend.
6. **Flag uncertainty.** If you could not test a skill properly that week, record `not assessed` rather than guessing. If a score sits between two bands, record both and explain.

## Descriptors by skill

### 1. Listening (comprensión auditiva)

| Band | What she can do |
|---|---|
| B1.1 | Follows the main points of clear, standard-accent speech on familiar topics (daily life, work, swimming) when delivery is fairly slow. |
| B1.2 | Gets the main points of radio or TV items on current affairs or her own interests when delivery is relatively clear. Can follow a short talk on a familiar subject. |
| B2.1 | Follows extended talk and lectures, including an argument with some complexity, if the topic is reasonably familiar. Understands most TV news and current affairs. |
| B2.2 | Understands most films in standard Spanish and native-speed conversation between others. Copes with some regional accents (Rioplatense, Caribbean, Andalusian) with effort. |

Suggested test material: RNE or Cadena SER clips, news podcasts, film scenes, interviews on human rights or sport.

### 2. Reading (comprensión lectora)

| Band | What she can do |
|---|---|
| B1.1 | Understands texts in everyday or work-related language and personal messages describing events and feelings. |
| B1.2 | Reads straightforward factual texts on her interests with satisfactory comprehension; can scan a longer text for specific information. |
| B2.1 | Reads articles and reports on contemporary issues where the writer takes a position or viewpoint. |
| B2.2 | Reads contemporary literary prose and specialised articles in her own fields (law, human rights) with occasional use of a dictionary. |

Suggested test material: El País or elDiario.es opinion pieces, NGO reports in Spanish, short fiction.

### 3. Spoken interaction (conversación)

| Band | What she can do |
|---|---|
| B1.1 | Deals with most situations likely to arise while travelling. Joins unprepared conversations on familiar topics. |
| B1.2 | Keeps a conversation going on familiar topics, gives brief reasons and opinions, checks and confirms information. |
| B2.1 | Interacts with enough fluency and spontaneity that regular conversation with native speakers is possible without strain on either side. Takes part in discussion and supports her views. |
| B2.2 | Explains a viewpoint on a topical issue, giving the advantages and disadvantages of options. Handles disagreement and changes of topic. |

### 4. Spoken production (expresión oral)

| Band | What she can do |
|---|---|
| B1.1 | Connects phrases simply to describe experiences, events, hopes and ambitions. |
| B1.2 | Gives brief reasons and explanations for opinions and plans; can narrate a story or the plot of a book or film and describe her reactions. |
| B2.1 | Gives clear, detailed descriptions on a wide range of subjects related to her interests. |
| B2.2 | Develops an argument with relevant supporting detail; could give a short structured talk on her work or a swim. |

### 5. Writing (expresión escrita)

| Band | What she can do |
|---|---|
| B1.1 | Writes simple connected text on familiar topics and personal letters or messages describing experiences. |
| B1.2 | Writes short structured texts (an email asking for something, a short summary) with clear sequencing. |
| B2.1 | Writes clear, detailed text on a range of subjects; can write an essay or report giving reasons for or against a point of view. |
| B2.2 | Writes letters and messages that highlight the personal significance of events, with appropriate register (tú/usted, formal/informal). |

## Cross-cutting qualities

Score these alongside the skills, using the same bands. They are drawn from the CEFR's qualitative aspects of spoken language use.

| Quality | B1 | B2 |
|---|---|---|
| **Range** | Enough language to get by, with some hesitation and circumlocution. | Enough range to give clear descriptions and express viewpoints without much searching for words; uses some complex sentence forms. |
| **Accuracy** | Reasonably accurate in familiar, predictable situations; errors do not usually block meaning. | Relatively high control; no errors that cause misunderstanding; can often correct herself. |
| **Fluency** | Keeps going comprehensibly, with noticeable pauses for planning and repair, especially in longer stretches. | Produces stretches of language at a fairly even tempo; few noticeably long pauses. |
| **Coherence** | Links a series of short elements into a connected sequence. | Uses a limited number of linking devices to make clear, coherent discourse. |

Grammar markers worth noting (from the Cervantes inventories, as evidence only, never as a quiz):
- **B1:** preterite vs imperfect in narrative, present subjunctive after common triggers (querer que, es importante que, cuando + future), conditional for polite requests.
- **B2:** imperfect subjunctive, si-clauses (si tuviera..., habría...), subjunctive in relative clauses, reported speech with tense shifts, a wider range of connectors (sin embargo, por lo tanto, a pesar de que).

Record whether she produces these spontaneously and correctly in real tasks, not whether she can explain them.

## Vocabulary target

Eva's plan is to reach the 4,000 most common Spanish words first (roughly B2), then the next 4,000.

- Track an estimated count of active and passive vocabulary against a frequency list. Say which list you are using; frequency lists differ.
- Report passive (recognised) and active (used unprompted) separately.
- Treat the 4,000-word figure as an approximate guide to B2, not a precise threshold.

## Monthly summary for Eva

Once a month, give Eva a short summary:
- Current band per skill, with the trend since the previous month.
- A concrete example from her own work that shows each improvement (e.g. a sentence she said in October that she could not have said in September).
- Which skills are holding back an overall B2, and what kind of material would help most (more listening, more writing, etc.).
- Any skill you have not been able to assess properly.

Keep it brief and evidence-led. Do not inflate progress.
